R-C-C: Role, Context, Constraints

Most weak prompts are one line long. R-C-C is a three-part checklist for fixing that. You won't need all three parts every time, but when an answer comes back generic, one of them is usually missing.

  1. R

    Role

    Tell the AI who to be. A tutor explains things differently than an editor does, and an editor works differently than someone writing quiz questions.

    "Act as a chemistry tutor for a 10th grader."

  2. C

    Context

    Explain what's going on: the class, the assignment, what you already understand, and where you get stuck. The AI only knows what you tell it.

    "I have a stoichiometry test Friday. Mole ratios make sense to me, but I keep messing up limiting reactants."

  3. C

    Constraints

    Set the rules for the answer, like format, length, difficulty, and what it shouldn't do. This is also where you make sure the work stays yours.

    "Give me 5 practice problems, easiest first. Put the answers at the very end so I can try them first."

Spot the hallucinated citation

You asked a chatbot for sources on bias in AI for a research paper, and it gave you these three. Two are real papers. One doesn't exist. Find the fake to earn the Prompt Crafter badge.

Citation Lab Pick the fake

"Give me three peer-reviewed sources about bias in AI for my research paper."

    How to check any citation
    1. Search the exact title, in quotes, on Google Scholar or your school library's database.
    2. Make sure the journal is real and has its own website.
    3. Open the DOI or link and confirm it goes to that exact paper.
    4. Skim the paper to check it says what the chatbot claimed. Real papers get misquoted too.
