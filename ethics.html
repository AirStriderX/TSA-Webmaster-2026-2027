Ethical AI & Media

AI can help you study, or it can land you in an honor-code meeting. It can also fake photos and voices well enough to fool people. This module covers where the lines are and how to catch a fake.

Using AI without cheating

Using AI isn't automatically cheating, and it isn't automatically fine. It depends on what your teacher allows for that specific assignment. Two classes at the same school can have completely different rules, so check every time, and ask before you start if the rules aren't clear.

Plagiarism means passing off someone else's words or ideas as your own. Many schools now treat turning in AI-written work the same way, even though no person wrote it. Rewording AI text so it sounds like you doesn't change that.

Usually fine, if your teacher allows AI

  • Brainstorming topics or thesis ideas, then choosing one and writing it yourself
  • Asking for an explanation of a concept you're stuck on
  • Having it quiz you before a test
  • Getting feedback on a draft and making the fixes yourself
  • Checking grammar on writing you already finished

Crosses the line

  • Turning in AI-written text as your own
  • Rewording AI text so your teacher won't notice
  • Using AI on a test or assignment that says no AI
  • Keeping a quote or source from AI without checking it's real
  • Making up data for a lab report

Tell your teacher what you used

When AI helps on an assignment and it's allowed, say so. MLA and APA both have formats for citing AI tools now, and a short note at the end works too:

I used ChatGPT to brainstorm three possible thesis statements and picked the second one. I wrote the essay myself and checked every fact against my sources.
AI detectors get it wrong

In a 2023 Stanford study, seven popular AI detectors flagged more than half of the essays written by non-native English speakers as AI-generated, on average. Those essays were written by people.

That's algorithmic bias: a tool built from data ends up treating one group unfairly. A detector score alone shouldn't be the proof that someone cheated.

Bias shows up in other AI too

The 2018 Gender Shades study tested commercial face-analysis software. It got the gender wrong for darker-skinned women up to 34.7% of the time, compared with 0.8% for lighter-skinned men. The researchers also found that two common face datasets used to test these systems were made up mostly of lighter-skinned faces.

Checks that work on real fakes

Zoom in on hands, teeth, ears, and jewelry, and read every bit of text in the image. Make sure the light and the shadows agree. Then look past the image itself. Who posted it first? Does a reverse image search turn up an older original? Does it carry Content Credentials, a label some cameras and AI tools attach to show where a file came from?

For a call or voicemail that sounds like someone you know, hang up and call them back on the number you already have saved.
